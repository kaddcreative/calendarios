Profile diagnosis

An analysis of what the profile communicates today, comparing the briefing Marina sent with the screenshots of her bio, highlights and grid.

Current positioning

The profile presents a specialist in lymphatic drainage and post-operative care with a lot of social proof: 850 posts, 5,845 followers, a consistent rosé identity, before and after photos with an individual-results notice, and a bio that already mentions years of experience, more than 10 thousand lives and the A.P® method. What weakens the perception is the mix of conversations in the same feed: posts for the end client, posts for fellow professionals and personal humor content. Services listed in the briefing, such as lipedema, cryolipolysis and facial cleansing, barely appear in the grid, so a first-time visitor takes a while to understand everything the Studio offers.

Critical blockers
  • Two audiences in one feed: the briefing speaks to women looking for care, but some posts are aimed at aesthetics professionals, like the one about the Pecquet cistern and the one about friends who do not support the business.
  • Strong clinical images without preparation: the post with the open wound near the navel and the video about the taping injury sit in the grid. They can scare someone who is still deciding about surgery. Use a neutral cover and a sensitive content notice.
  • Conflicting years of experience: the briefing says 12 years, the bio says 13 and one video mentions 12 years in the clinic. Standardize to "over 12 years" until Marina confirms the exact number.
  • Briefing services with low visibility: lipedema, complications care, cryolipolysis and facial cleansing hardly show up in posts or in the profile name, which only mentions drainage and post-op care.
  • Too many before and afters in the grid: social proof is a strength, but several in a row turn the feed into a body showcase. Keep the individual-results notice, keep client authorization and mix in educational content.
  • Result comparisons: the post with "without Marina" and "with Marina" can sound like a promise. Replace it with supportive wording, such as "before and after X sessions".
  • Location highlight in the middle of the row: the highlight with the location pin exists, in fifth position. Moving it to the first positions helps visitors see Savassi and the address right away.
  • Followers to following ratio: 5,845 followers against 2,600 following suggests part of the base came from follow-backs, which tends to lower organic reach. Worth confirming in Insights.
Visible audience pains
  • "I am so swollen after surgery."
  • "Is what I have lipedema or cellulite?"
  • "I do not know how many drainage sessions I need."
  • "I want a safe place to take care of my body."
Hidden audience pains
  • Fear of complications after surgery.
  • Shame about the body and pressure to match a standard.
  • The feeling of having tried everything without understanding what happens in the body.
  • Worry about spending money on someone who does not offer safety.
Emotional desires
  • To feel heard and welcomed.
  • To recognize her own body and feel good in it.
  • To know she is in good hands.
Practical desires
  • Recovery guided by someone who understands post-op care.
  • A clear session plan with a realistic timeline.
  • A reserved time slot and care without rushing.
  • Care that respects the doctor's instructions.
Objections that hold back the booking decision
"Will it work for my case?"01

The logic behind it: doubt about individual results. Content needs to explain how the assessment works and that results are individual.

"I am afraid of pain or of making my recovery worse"02

The logic behind it: insecurity about an operated body. Content needs to show medical clearance, gentle technique and close follow-up.

"Another professional charges less"03

The logic behind it: comparison by price. Content needs to show what Marina delivers beyond the session: assessment, method, safety and years of experience.

"I do not know how many sessions I will need"04

The logic behind it: fear of spending without an end date. Content needs to explain that the plan is built after a conversation and an assessment of the case.

"I do not have my doctor's clearance yet"05

The logic behind it: doubt about the right moment to start. Content needs to cover the surgeon's clearance and how to plan before surgery.

Market opportunity

Few professionals combine lymphatic drainage, post-operative care, lipedema and body aesthetics with a welcoming voice and a stated focus on safety. Marina has experience, a method, testimonials and a recognizable visual identity. The opportunity for the next 30 days is to organize the feed by service, separate the conversation with clients from the conversation with colleagues and lead every post to a WhatsApp chat.

Tone by format

Reels

A 7-second reel, one take. How to shoot it: film a single scene of about 7 seconds, with no cuts and nobody talking to the camera. The hook goes in as on-screen text, written like a direct headline, with upbeat music in the background. The caption is what converts.

Want to record yourself talking? Use the caption text as your script! It was written to build authority and convert!

Carousel

Educational, one short idea per slide, simple and welcoming language, closing with an invitation to save or message on WhatsApp.

Static post

Main phrase highlighted, little text and the rosé palette the profile has already built.

Stories

Studio behind the scenes, question boxes about drainage and post-op care and reposts of clients with authorization.

Always do

  • Write the hook as a direct headline, with the subject clear from the first words.
  • Cover one service per post and point to WhatsApp (31) 99591-3617 in most pieces.
  • Mention the surgeon's clearance whenever the topic is post-op care.
  • Publish before and afters only with authorization and the individual-results notice.

Never do

  • Promise guaranteed results, a cure or weight loss.
  • Give a diagnosis or medical advice: diagnosis always belongs to the doctor.
  • Mix talk to clients and talk to fellow professionals in the same post.
  • Publish a strong clinical image without a neutral cover and a notice.
